# Stage 6 — Competing candidate layouts

Both drawings use the same illustrative room areas from stage 7. Rotating or mirroring either drawing remains equally possible because no compass bearing or exterior footprint for this apartment was established.

## Candidate A — bedroom opens to kitchen (favored)

[Open vector plan](../08-output/candidate-A.svg).

- Apartment entry leads to an L-shaped corridor with storage, bath, toilet, living room and kitchen entrances.
- The bedroom's pictured door leads to the kitchen. The kitchen therefore has two connections: bedroom and corridor.
- Living room and bedroom occupy the wider, windowed edge; kitchen's window faces another courtyard-facing wall or recess.
- Main supporting cue: the distinctive kitchen tile visible beyond the bedroom door in [08](../../images/08.webp).
- Main weakness: none of [09–11](../../images/09.webp) explicitly shows the bedroom-side kitchen door. A wall section is outside their combined views.

## Candidate B — bedroom opens to a tiled corridor spur

[Open vector plan](../08-output/candidate-B.svg).

- The bedroom opens directly to a short tiled corridor segment. Kitchen has a corridor entrance and shares a wall with the bedroom, without a direct connecting door.
- This interpretation keeps both rooms connected only to the corridor, a common layout prior, but no plan for this building confirms it.
- Main weakness: the tiled corridor spur appears in no hall photograph. [12](../../images/12.webp) and [16](../../images/16.webp) instead show wood-look corridor flooring. A mixed floor finish is possible, but is an additional assumption.

## Selection

**A is the working candidate**, based on the patterned tile match and the photographed wood-look floor outside the kitchen's other door. This is a comparative judgment, not a calibrated 70–90% probability. A repeatable brick-building section could favor B, but no series or section plan has been verified for this address. Both arrangements satisfy the listing and area totals. Exact bath/toilet/storage order, left/right orientation, window alignment, and room dimensions remain free variables.

The SVGs deliberately show one compact orthogonal realization of each graph. Many other shapes and placements satisfy the same evidence. A future generator should enumerate mirrored/rotated forms and corridor arrangements, then ask a user to select among them.
